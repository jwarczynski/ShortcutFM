Text Generation with

Motivation

Generating text fast — without losing quality

  • Autoregressive models: one token at a time — great quality, high latency.
  • Non-autoregressive (NAR): all tokens in parallel — quality penalty.
  • Diffusion-based NAR: better quality, but 100s–1000s of denoising steps → high latency.

The question

Can we build a non-autoregressive model that generates high-quality text in a single denoising step?

Background

Flow Matching — a simpler alternative to diffusion

Define a straight-line path from noise to the target embedding:

$$z_t = (1 - t)\,z_0 + t\,z_1, \quad z_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I), \; z_1 \sim p_{\text{data}}$$

Train $f_\theta$ to match the ground-truth velocity at every interpolated point — this is the flow-matching loss:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{FM}} = \mathbb{E}\,\big\|\,f_\theta(z_t, t) - (z_1 - z_0)\,\big\|^2$$

Generate by integrating $n$ small Euler steps from random noise.

The inference trajectory is curved — the model averages over many conditional paths.

The Problem

One step → one big leap into the wrong place

  • Flow matching could sample in one step:  $z_1 \approx z_0 + f_\theta(z_0, 0)$
  • But $f_\theta$ only knows the local instantaneous velocity at $z_0$.
  • Because the real trajectory curves, the one-step prediction overshoots — landing far from any real text embedding.
  • This is why prior NAR continuous-diffusion approaches use, e.g., 2000 steps to be competitive.

We need a model that knows where the trajectory is going, not just where it's pointing at the current moment.

Our idea

Teach the model to take shortcuts

We extend the network with a step-size argument $d$:

$$s_\theta(z_t,\; t,\; d)$$
  • $d \to 0$: behaves like standard flow matching — local velocity.
  • $d > 0$: predicts the average direction over a step of that size, accounting for the curve ahead.
  • Adapted from Frans et al., ICLR 2025 (computer vision) — first time, to our knowledge, applied to text generation.

Key Property

Self-consistency: one step of $2d$ = two steps of $d$

Train the model so that one step of size $2d$ matches two steps of size $d$ — the self-consistency loss:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SC}} = \big\|\,s_\theta(z_t,t,2d)\;-\;\tfrac{1}{2}\big(s_\theta(z_t,t,d)+s_\theta(z_{t+d},t+d,d)\big)\,\big\|^2$$
  • A recursion bootstraps shortcuts of any size from local velocity.
  • Lets one model serve all step counts — train once, decode at $1$, $2$, $4$, … steps.

Takeaways

What we showed

  • Shortcut flow matching adapts cleanly from vision to text generation.
  • In one denoising step, BLEU more than doubles on two of three datasets vs. the flow-matching baseline at the same step count.
  • On PAWS-Wiki we even match a Transformer AR baseline in a single forward pass.
  • One model serves all step counts. No retraining for different decoding budgets.
